Can the optimization problem itself serve as the policy?

We formulate quadratic programs as end-to-end differentiable policy representations, unifying model-based control and robot learning while retaining structure, constraints, and interpretability.

Abstract

We present an end-to-end QP-based policy framework that enables systematic policy construction with minimal domain-specific design, while preserving the transparency and interpretability of model-based control. The proposed policy representation supports both domain-randomized model-based auto-tuning, where policy parameters are optimized over distributions of disturbances and model variations, and black-box policy construction, where the problem is formulated in terms of a possibly unknown model without requiring explicit notions of states, inputs, or the underlying system dynamics.

We establish connections to robust control, multilayer perceptrons, and robot learning, and validate the framework in simulation and hardware across robustness, automatic policy tuning, and control under unknown dynamics.

Simulation experiments

Lunar landing video placeholder

Robust lunar landing

A predictive QP controller is trained across a fleet of vehicles with uncertain initial conditions and slosh dynamics. Domain randomization reduces tail errors: the learned controller’s worst landing error is less than one third of the nominal controller’s largest error.

Cartpole video placeholder

Black-box cartpole control

Projection-QP policies learn nonlinear swing-up behavior directly from input-output rollouts, without requiring an explicit model or predefined notions of system state and input.

Hardware experiment

Crazyflie crosswind video placeholder

A Crazyflie quadrotor flies through a 5 m/s crosswind generated by an industrial fan. The framework jointly tunes the structured controller and disturbance observer from randomized rollouts. The learned QP policy converges faster than manually tuned MPC baselines and drives steady-state position error to zero.
